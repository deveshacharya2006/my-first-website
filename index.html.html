The Furnace That
Never Cools

Tucked into Sodagaran Mohalla near Rakhi House, this is one of Jodhpur's old-city institutions — running for roughly eighty years on the strength of one red-brick bhatti. That furnace is where the shop gets its second name, "Lal Bhatti Wale," and where every kachori, every jalebi and every laddoo on the counter is still fried in pure desi ghee, the old way.

काउंटर के अंदरInside the Glass Counter

A look at what's actually sitting in the display case on any given morning — tray after tray, sorted the way the shop has always sorted them.

बंगाली मिठाई

Boondi laddoo, soft white rasgulla, orange rasmalai and Bengali mithai — stacked tray by tray, synced in sugar syrup.

मावा की मिठाई

Mawa ki mithai — khoya pressed into barfi, peda rolled by hand, and jalebi coiled straight off the bhatti.

घर की खासियत

घर की खासियतHouse Specialties

मोगर कचौरी
Mogar Kachori

Baked moong dal, besan and a mix of spices sealed inside a flaky shell, then deep-fried in pure desi ghee until it blisters golden. Best with red or green chutney.

बारीक तार जलेबी
Fine-Thread Jalebi

Fermented batter piped into threads finer than the usual jalebi, coiled directly into hot ghee and soaked while still warm in saffron-scented syrup.

गोंद गिरी लड्डू
Gond Giri Laddoo

Roasted singhara (water chestnut) flour bound with edible gum and grated coconut, rolled with cardamom and a generous handful of chopped dry fruits.

स्पेशल नमकीन
Special Namkeen

The shop's own savoury blend — a Lal Bhatti recipe fried fresh in small batches, the way it's been done here for decades.
